Bryer Manwell is an environmental engineer and geoscientist in British Columbia who specializes in hydrogeology and spends her days on the hard septic jobs: sites close to wells, lakes and creeks where a standard system is not enough. She owns CaulWell Engineering and Geoscience Ltd., and she joined me to talk about what septic systems do to the water around them.

If you install, pump or maintain septic systems, you will hear how Bryer traces a septic plume by walking the shoreline and digging holes, why a system can pass its treatment standard and still send nitrate into the groundwater, and how a passive barrier of wood and sand can clean it up. She also explains why the best thing you can do for a customer on a well is get the water tested, and shares the five habits she says keep her going.

What we cover:
- How an environmental engineer and geoscientist ended up working on septic systems
- What septic nitrate does to wells, lakes and algae
- Why advanced treatment units can still send nitrate downstream
- How she traces a septic plume by walking the shoreline
- Where the hot spots are: density, soil and distance to water
- Permeable reactive barriers: a passive fix for nitrate
- A ten-year system serving 35 homes, and what went wrong
- Should you drink reverse osmosis water? Test your well first
- What installers and maintenance providers can do, and her courses
- Her million dollar advice: five pillars of health
